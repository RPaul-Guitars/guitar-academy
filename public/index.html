Why This Order

Every real guitarist's development follows the same spine: your fretting hand learns shapes before it learns theory, your ear develops in parallel with your hands rather than after, and rhythm is trained explicitly rather than assumed. This plan sequences five disciplines — ear training, rhythm, chord progressions, fretboard fluency, and soloing/improvisation — so each month's work makes the next month easier, the same arc used in conservatory method books and by working session players.

Daily Split (45–60 min)

  • 10 min — Ear training reps
  • 10 min — Metronome / rhythm drill
  • 15 min — This month's chord or scale material
  • 15 min — Fretboard fluency or improvisation
  • 5 min — Log the session

Chord Shape Finder

Pick a root, a chord quality, and a shape category — it finds every place on the neck that shape actually works, computed from the actual notes rather than a fixed diagram library. Open chords only show up where a real open-position shape exists; barre, power, and triad shapes are movable, so you'll often see several positions up and down the neck.

Fretboard Note Trainer

Full 22-fret neck. A dot lands somewhere on the board — name the note before you move on. Easy stays in frets 0–5 with a 4-choice keypad; Moderate is the full 12-note keypad and requires mastering frets 0–12 before unlocking 13–22; Difficult opens the whole neck immediately. Every position you identify correctly stays lit up on the board across sessions, in every difficulty.
